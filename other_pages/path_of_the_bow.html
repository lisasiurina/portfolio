02. Physical Constraints & Hardware Logic

The Physical Constraint

Stringing a real bow takes leverage—you brace the limb against your foot and use your body weight to bend the wood. Standard hand controllers can't track lower-body position, and mapping this to a button press or skipping it entirely would make the whole thing feel weightless and fake.

Implementation

To track the stance, the setup uses an HTC Vive Tracker strapped to the player's foot, linked to a Full-Body IK avatar.

The stringing logic runs every frame in C#. The attach state is gated by two simultaneous Vector3.Distance checks: hand-to-notch proximity and controller-to-foot-tracker distance. The string only locks once the player physically lowers the bow frame to meet their actual foot position.

03. Diegetic Onboarding

In-World Visual Guides

Most people don't know the traditional bow-assembly process or the foot-bracing technique. Floating tutorial boxes or UI overlays would break the realism of the interaction.

To keep the instruction fully diegetic, illustrated wall scrolls demonstrating the assembly steps and foot placement are hung directly around the workspace, serving as an intuitive reference in the environment.

04. Retrospective

Designing for Hardware Scope

A foot-mounted Vive Tracker made posture-based interaction possible by providing the player’s actual foot position. With a standard headset-and-controllers setup, the mechanic would require an approximation based on controller height or a simpler proximity check. Hardware limitations shape the interaction itself.

Diegetic Guidance vs. UX Clarity

Wall scrolls kept the assembly instructions within the environment. However, diegetic guidance is only effective when players can actually notice and understand it. If an essential step remains unclear, a brief UI prompt is better than a hidden mechanic. Visual consistency should never come at the expense of clarity.
